Routing under a budget, on 387,403 real Kafka events

Run 1 of E1, now under amendment after independent review. Does a budgeted router with a rules floor, per-entity deviation scoring and guards admit the events humans later treated as urgent? On Apache Kafka, 2022–2026, it does not: the rules alone over-fill a 2 % budget and the deviation layer never finds a positive.

Independent review (2026-09-06): the numbers reproduce, the evidence does not yet stand

A fresh reviewer re-derived every headline figure below to eight decimals and then found two blockers in the evaluation itself. One labelling function needs release-state fields that no event carries, yet it cast 145,425 confident negative votes. And the text that rules, features and labels scan comes from export-time snapshots (JIRA summaries, comment bodies, PR titles, final components), so timestamps alone cannot prove it was known at decision time. Six further findings: the label model's symmetric negative voting makes urgency a conjunction of rare outcomes and 0.95 is its accuracy cap; the declared-priority rule misses → Critical transitions; [VOTE] fires on every reply; lateness secondaries use row-index distance; the human sanity sample and remediation record are missing.

Mechanism one: the rules floor eats the budget

The rules floor is meant to be a small, mandatory set of obviously urgent events. On Kafka it flags 2.32 % of all events, more than the whole 2 % budget in 33 of 52 months. Because rule admissions are mandatory, R5 has zero slots left for deviation admissions in those months and becomes identical to R1.

Rule hits are only about 3× more likely to be revealed-urgent than other events (0.36 % vs 0.12 %). Every message in a [VOTE] thread, every dependency bump mentioning a CVE and every "not a blocker" aside counts. The floor is neither small nor precise here; it is a broad keyword filter that happens to be mandatory.

Mechanism two: the guards block the deviation layer

Above 5 % the budget is no longer binding: R5 leaves most of its slots empty. What stops it is the pair of guards designed to keep bursts from amplifying: an absolute-volume floor (≥ 3 events in the entity's 5-minute bucket) and multi-window confirmation. On Kafka's long-lived contributor and component baseline keys, the median occupied 5-minute bucket holds one event.

Of 378,421 rule-negative events, 0.74 % ever become eligible at the 10 % budget (GitHub 1.16 %, dev@ 0.92 %, JIRA 0.06 %). The admitted set is dominated by GitHub review bursts (2,435 of 2,787), which the labels do not consider urgent. None of the 2,787 is a positive.

The scorer itself carries little signal. R4 is the same per-entity HBOS with the guards removed and the full budget available; it sits at or below random at every budget. R2 uses the identical feature vector with one global baseline and is 1.5–3× random. Per-entity keying, the design's central bet, hurts rather than helps on this corpus. The production classifier (R3, gap-only robust-z) is indistinguishable from random.

What "urgent" meant: revealed urgency from what humans did afterwards

Labels are derived, never opined. Twelve labelling functions look only at events after each candidate (committer replied within an hour, priority raised later, PR reverted within 48 h, …) and are fused by a weighted label model. All twelve pass the pre-registered gates: estimated accuracy ≥ 0.6 and coverage ≥ 1 %. The trunk-CI function was excluded by a registered amendment because the GitHub API stream carries no CI events.

What this means, and what happens next

For the thesis. C1 as stated is falsified on Kafka at b = 2 %, and the deviation layer contributes nothing at any budget up to 10 %. The defensible central statement: on a real open-source engineering stream, revealed urgency is rare (≈ 0.1 %), the rules floor alone over-fills a 2 % budget with mostly non-urgent [VOTE] / CVE / "blocker" mentions, and per-entity burst anomalies are uncorrelated with what humans later treated as urgent. That is a negative result with a clear mechanism, and it motivates testing the batch lane (E2–E4) independently of the router.

For the design. Two changes follow, to be tested as new pre-registered policies rather than by re-tuning R5 on these months: make the rules floor budgeted rather than mandatory (rank rule hits, admit up to a share of b); and drop the volume floor and multi-window guards on low-density entities, or key baselines on thread / issue where density exists. The production classifier should not be upgraded to R5.

Open items before evidentiary status. Independent review of the label model, rule regexes, guard implementation and temporal firewall (in progress; blockers will be appended to the repo report as dated amendments). Human sanity sample. Metric remediation record. The registered secondary 2019–2026 run (needs ≈ 24 GB free memory). Flink replication. Ten attribution case studies: the current attribution file is empty because no HBOS term ever fired on a true positive.
