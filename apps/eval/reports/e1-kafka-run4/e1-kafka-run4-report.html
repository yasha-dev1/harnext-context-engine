What to take into the paper

  • C1 is falsified on Corpus R (runs 1, 3, 4 agree): per-entity deviation scoring with guards admits nothing useful at any budget; without guards it ranks urgent events below average.
  • The detector matters less than the keying. Three of four global detectors beat random by 2–4× at 2 %; HBOS and IsolationForest are statistically tied. Both LOFs, global and per-entity, are at random. Local-density methods do not find urgency in this feature space.
  • The signal is source-specific and lives in JIRA. Splitting the budget per source (R10) costs recall at 1–5 % and only pays at 10 %. A better design is to spend the fast-lane budget where the scorer works and let the batch lane carry the rest.
  • The ceiling is low. The best scorer recovers 9 % of later-urgent events at 2 % with under 1 % precision, roughly 100 interruptions per urgent one. Unsupervised deviation is not enough; the next registered step is a supervised weak-label ranker trained on prior months, which measures how much signal the features hold at all.
  • Proxy labels are still unvalidated. The human sanity sample is the gate; until two annotators fill it in, every number here is descriptive.

What changed between run 3 and run 4

  • R8 (rules outside the budget) and R9 (any-key guards) removed: identical to R5 in every admission.
  • R10 per-source global HBOS: one HBOS per source on the trailing 12 months; each event ranked by the percentile of its raw score within its source's training distribution. Unseen sources fall back to the global model.
  • R11–R13: R2 with IsolationForest (100 trees, seeded), ECOD (own O(log n) implementation of the empirical-CDF tail score) and LOF (k = 20, standardised, novelty mode). COPOD was dropped as a near-duplicate of ECOD on this feature vector; global LOF was added to test whether keying is what makes R6 weak (it is not).
  • Registered contrasts updated; the metric-remediation design condition and the human-sample source are R10.
  • Engineering: the run's 18.6 M score rows were OOM-killed at 37.6 GB on the first attempt; each month is now spilled to parquet as it completes and the final file is assembled by streaming.
